Profile

I work with leaders, legal, HR, and People teams to plan internal communications, explain change, and keep employees informed without burying them.   1. A national distribution network

    Field research, five sites

    Total rewards audit

    The company was reviewing its total rewards to find out whether employees were actually using the programs it paid for. I visited five sites and ran group interviews with ten to twenty people at a time, asking what they understood about the insurance and benefits available to them.

    Nearly everyone I spoke to was unwired, meaning warehouse staff without regular computer access at work. The programs were available to them on paper but hard to use in practice. Hours varied, the language around insurance took real effort to understand, and learning about any of it meant giving up unpaid personal time. Acting on it meant stepping away from a job where the work doesn’t stop.

    Employees who looked into it anyway often waited on hold for a long time and came away more confused, which a warehouse shift doesn’t leave room for. Some had given up. Others, mostly younger employees, had turned down coverage without ever having anyone on site to talk it through with. The employees who did know the programs well had specific, useful criticism, and I made sure it reached the employer.

    An HR presence at every site would have solved much of this, but it was never going to be funded. So I focused the recommendations on what could change, which was how information reached people in the first place and which channels worked for each part of the workforce.

    The review started as a question about how many people were using the programs. The fieldwork showed that low use came from friction in a benefits process designed for a workday the warehouse staff didn’t have.

    Summary Field research with frontline populations, audience segmentation, and recommendations scoped to what the organization could realistically do.

Even at this size, much of the work is deciding what people need to hear and when. Because I write every week, the standards get tested against real work instead of sitting in a document.

    Summary Brand voice and messaging from zero, editorial standards built to work for other writers, and AI-assisted drafting with a person accountable for every word.

  3. A multinational financial services firm

    Employee sentiment

    Reframing research findings for executives

    I designed and ran interviews with the leadership team, then pulled the responses together into findings the executive sponsor could act on.

    The first read-out didn’t land the way it needed to. It carried so much detail from the interviews that the conversation drifted toward individual comments instead of the decisions the team had to make.

    So I rebuilt it around themes and what they meant for the business, at the level the sponsor worked at, and organized it around what the company could do next.

    Summary Interview design and synthesis, executive stakeholder management, and knowing when findings are being heard as a list instead of a direction.
